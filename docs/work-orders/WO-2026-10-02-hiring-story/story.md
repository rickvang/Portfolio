# Multi Product Integrations

Shared patterns for teams designing across multiple applications.

## A recurring problem across products

Across different engagements, I saw teams solving similar problems in separate applications. Many designers were doing work that could have been shared, while workflows were being defined repeatedly across products. I wanted to help turn those recurring decisions into patterns that teams could reuse. This story brings together that work, with a real-estate engagement as one example.

## My contribution within a shared effort

In the real-estate engagement, I was a primary contributor to defining workflow patterns and representing them in the system. I worked with three teammates. We took on different aspects of the patterns and iterated together. The library reflected that shared effort, and my contribution included both the pattern definitions and a reusable layout starter.

## The decisions teams were repeating

The overlap showed up in recurring layout and interaction decisions. The pattern work covered several concrete areas:

### Close buttons, modals, and drawers

The definitions covered close-button placement and the use of modals and drawers across workflows.

### Layout and reading order

We defined layout guidance around reading order, such as top to bottom and left to right.

### Labels and values

The patterns addressed how label/value pairs were presented horizontally or vertically.

## Learning from other applications

Some of the other applications had more sophisticated dashboards and rules for progressive disclosure. Comparing workflows with those teams helped us identify scenarios where their process fit better than our own. We adopted those approaches and brought them into the shared system. Record creation and viewing was one example of this collaboration.

## How the shared work took shape

The pattern definitions took shape in Figma component libraries with guidelines. Designers could use these shared artifacts when working across applications:

### Figma component libraries

Reusable components represented the layout and interaction patterns we defined together.

### Guidelines

The libraries were accompanied by guidance on the shared workflow and layout patterns.

### A layout starter

I created a system layout structure that designers could use as a starter template when working in a different problem space.

## A shared pattern for creating and viewing records

Creating and viewing records was one workflow where we adopted an existing approach. The change connected a specific interaction decision to our broader pattern work:

### Earlier approach: page refresh and drill-down

As I recall, the earlier flow used a page refresh and drill-down, influenced by development constraints at the time.

### Adopted approach: create and view in slide-ins

We adopted another application's approach to creating or viewing a record in a slide-in, and brought that workflow into our shared system.

### Work through implementation feasibility

Across the pattern work, I collaborated with frontend developers to identify what could and could not be built. I contributed the pattern definitions and Figma work; the frontend team implemented the code.

## Adoption across the projects I worked on

The libraries were generally adopted across the different projects I worked on. The work was well accepted by the team, with adaptation challenges among more established teams. The shared system included the Figma libraries and guidelines, my layout starter, and pattern definitions shaped by collaboration with other teams.
