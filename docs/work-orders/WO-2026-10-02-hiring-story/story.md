# Multi Product Integrations

Shared patterns for teams designing across multiple applications.

## The same work, repeated across apps

Across different engagements, I saw designers solving similar problems in separate applications. Teams repeated workflows and design work that could have been shared. This story brings together that work, with a real-estate engagement as one example.

## A shared starting point

In the real-estate engagement, I was a primary contributor to the workflow patterns. I worked with three teammates, and we iterated together on different parts of the system. Our work took shape as Figma component libraries and guidelines. I also created a starter layout structure that designers could use in different problem spaces. The patterns covered everyday decisions such as close-button placement, when to use a modal or drawer, and how to display labels and values.

## Learning from established teams

The team supported creating the shared system, but established teams found it harder to adapt their existing processes. We worked together to compare approaches. When another team's process fit a scenario better than ours, we adopted it and brought it into the system.

## From the library to the products

I worked with frontend developers to understand what could and could not be built, while the frontend team implemented the code. The libraries were generally adopted across the projects I worked on. They gave designers shared patterns and a layout to start from, with guidance they could use as they worked through each project's needs.
