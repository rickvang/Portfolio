# AI council for product decisions

I built an AI council to examine product ideas through executive, manager and worker perspectives. Each persona assessed the same question against its own work and pain points.

## Questioning a product idea

I wanted to explore whether a product idea would help an executive, a manager or a worker on the ground. Each would encounter the same idea from a different position, with different responsibilities and pain points. I created the council to bring those perspectives into product discovery.

## Giving each role a working context

I built role profiles from industry practices and whitepapers. They connected the work people do, the decisions they own and what would make a tool useful or hard to trust. A separate Minnesota contractor profile generated with the same instructions shows what that looked like.

- **Executive:** Compiled AI persona
- **Manager:** Compiled AI persona
- **Worker:** Compiled AI persona
- **Work from lead to closeout:** Site visit, estimate, proposal, construction, change orders, inspection and closeout.
- **Decisions he owns:** Which jobs to bid, pricing and margin, and crew and subcontractor selection.
- **What earns trust:** Fast entry from the field, no duplicate data entry and a clear trail for approvals.
- **Question to test with contractors:** What gets lost between you, the client, the crew and subcontractors?

## The early answers explained usefulness

Before the personas were in place, the AI reasoned about the request and explained how it would be useful. After I added the framework, the answers evaluated the request against each role’s workload and pain points. They could tell me whether it was useful from that perspective at all.

- **Before the persona framework:** Reason about the request and explain how it would be useful.
- **With the persona framework:** Evaluate the request against the role’s workload and pain points, including whether it is useful at all.

## Same question, different judgments

Each AI persona answered through its compiled role profile. Responsibilities, workload and pain points shaped the responses, so the same question produced different perspectives. I could compare whether the idea seemed useful to each role and how each thought about the data.

- **Shared question:** Would this product idea be useful to you in your work?

Illustrative priorities for each AI role:

- **Executive:** Business value, strategic priorities and organizational risk.
- **Manager:** Team performance, resource allocation and coordination.
- **Worker:** Completing daily tasks, practical tools and avoiding extra work.

## What changed for product and UX

The council helped me look past a general ‘this could be useful’ answer. I could ask whose work the idea actually served, where it might add friction and what each role needed from the data. I brought those perspectives together in a use-case scenario.

- **Product direction:** Before treating an idea as a priority, I could compare its value by role and identify what still needed research. That gave scope discussions a clearer basis than general usefulness.
- **UX direction:** Core and occasional tasks, decision rights and trust needs gave the scenario a structure: what each role needed to see, what they could act on and where more context was needed.


## Keeping assumptions visible

The generator separated evidence from assumptions, assigned confidence levels and identified questions for research with real users.

- **High:** Strong evidence from direct research or multiple reliable internal sources
- **Medium:** Some evidence from internal materials, SMEs or customer-facing teams
- **Low:** Mostly inferred or synthetic
- **Unknown:** Not enough evidence to support the claim
